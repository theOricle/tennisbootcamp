## The voice in one line

A world-class coach speaking plainly to a motivated player.

Not a salesperson. Not a tech company. Not a hype account. The coach has nothing to prove and no reason to oversell — the training speaks, the copy just tells you what happens, what it costs, and what you leave with.

## The four traits (from brand.md)

- **Welcoming** — direct address ("you", "your game"), no gatekeeping tone, no jargon a first-time player wouldn't know.
- **Serious** — statements, not exclamations. Commitments ("we'll reach out within a day"), not vibes.
- **Athletic** — the language of training: reps, court, level, schedule, block, session. Verbs over adjectives.
- **Premium** — restraint. Short sentences. No begging for the sale, no urgency theatrics, no emoji.

## Hard rules

1. **No filler intensifiers.** "Real", "really", "truly", "actually", "very", "super" almost never earn their place. Owner precedent: he cut "Real reps. Real progress." from the hero as unclear filler — "real" is a superfluous word. If the sentence survives without the intensifier, delete the intensifier. (Exception: an intensifier that carries a factual contrast may stay, but the default is delete.)
2. **Mechanics always explicit.** Never compress a money or policy mechanic into shorthand a first-time reader could misread. Owner precedent: "credited to your first program" was banned; the required phrasing spells it out — *the assessment costs $20, and if you enroll in a program afterward that $20 comes off the price.* Short surfaces (hero microcopy, meta descriptions) may tighten it, but never below "join a program after and it comes off the price." The same rule applies to refunds, make-ups, and membership: say the number, the condition, and who pays whom.
3. **Concrete over clever.** A specific fact beats a slogan: "Up to 8 players a group" beats "small groups you'll love"; "20 minutes on court with the coach" beats "a personalized evaluation experience." Cleverness is allowed only when it lands on something true and specific ("Twenty minutes decides the next season of your game" earns its place because the mechanic sits right beside it).
4. **No salesperson hype.** No manufactured scarcity ("limited spots!"), no empty flattery ("you've got a strong profile"), no "unlock", "supercharge", "elevate", "take it to the next level". A single factual scarcity line ("2 spots left", computed from data) is fine; invented urgency is not.
5. **No SaaS-speak.** No "journey", "experience" (as a noun for a product), "seamless", "solution", "platform", "empower". A player has a game and a schedule, not a journey.
6. **No stale promises.** Copy must describe the funnel as it works today: groups are open to join, and the quiz is the side path (owner, 2026-11-16). A player picks the group that fits their age and level and joins it; a player who is not sure which group takes the 2-minute quiz and our coaches place them. Each open block is its own group card, and its button reads "Enroll" and opens the block's sign-up page, where the player picks who plays, signs the agreement and pays (owner, 2026-11-19: "you should be able to sign up and pay for a group not just ask to join"; the card's button has payment behind it, owner 2026-11-18; "Enroll", owner 2026-11-20: "looks right but maybe enroll instead? your choice"); a group with no open block reads "Dates coming soon" and offers "Notify me" (owner, 2026-11-20). The coach confirms the level on court in week one (see Joining flow below). High Performance is by approval: its "Ask to join" opens the same sign-up page, whose approval step is the quiz, then our coaches' approval. The 20-minute on-court assessment is optional, for anyone who wants certainty before paying, and never a step every player must take (owner 2026-10-02, 2026-10-12). Never say or imply that every player starts with, or is placed by, the assessment, or that the quiz is a step every player must take. A claim the product no longer delivers is a voice failure even if the sentence is pretty.
7. **Consistent product vocabulary.** "Join a bootcamp" (the main button: the lime one on every public page, to `/programs`; owner 2026-11-17, and it read "Join a group" until 2026-11-17; the label lives once, `JOIN_CTA_LABEL` in `src/lib/mainAction.ts`) · "Take the 2-minute quiz" (the quiz link, exact label; it opens the intake wizard and is never a lime button: the side line reads "Not sure which group? Take the 2-minute quiz" for everyone signed out, whatever their browser once sent, and "Your quiz answers are in." with "Open your dashboard" only for a signed-in account with quiz answers on file: a browser cannot tell who is at it, owner 2026-11-18) · "Book Your Assessment" (the optional assessment button, exact casing) · "the 2-minute quiz" (the intake wizard) · "cohort" (a scheduled program group) · "enroll" (never "enrol"/"register" in body copy) · "Browse Programs" (an outline link to the catalog, where a page keeps one) · "our coaches" / "your coach" / "the coaching team" (who places, sets levels and confirms payments; see Staff wording below) · "Open to join", "{n} spots left", "Full" and "By approval" (a group's chip; "By approval" is also how High Performance is joined) · "Enroll", "Ask to join" and "Notify me" (a group card's one button, owner 2026-11-19 and 2026-11-20: "Enroll" on an open block, to its sign-up page; "Ask to join" on a by-approval block, to the same page; "Notify me" on a full block and on a group with no open block) · "Dates coming soon" (where a group with no open block shows its dates) · "provisional" (a level from the quiz or a placement, until our coaches confirm it on court in week one). One name per thing, everywhere, including emails.
8. **Exclamation marks: effectively zero.** The tagline "Where Athletes Evolve!" is the single grandfathered exception. Nothing else gets one.

## Surface-specific notes

- **Buttons**: verb-first, outcome-named. "Join a bootcamp" (the main button), "Book Your Assessment" (the one label for every assessment button, /assessment included), "Continue to payment", "Request a time". A button names what the click does: a group card's "Enroll" opens its block's sign-up page, where the player picks who plays, signs the agreement and pays (owner, 2026-11-19). It read "Join" until the owner's word, 2026-11-20: "looks right but maybe enroll instead? your choice". "Enroll" names that step, is the site's own word for it ("enroll in a program afterward", "You're enrolled", "Enrollment Confirmed"), and no longer repeats the main button's "Join a bootcamp", which only opens the groups; "join" stays where it means joining a bootcamp in general ("Join a bootcamp", "Ask to join", "Open to join"). A button that opens the quiz never reads "Join" or "Enroll" (owner, 2026-11-18). No "Submit"-style labels on marketing surfaces (wizard-final "Submit" inside the quiz is acceptable chrome).
- **Validation & errors**: state what's wrong and the one way out. Always offer info@tennisbootcamp.ca as the human fallback on submit failures. Never blame the user.
- **Empty states**: say what's true and what happens next ("Cohort dates aren't public yet." then the next step: take the 2-minute quiz, or leave your email). Never a dead end.
- **Emails**: greet by first name, one idea per paragraph, sign off "See you on the court," then "The Tennis Bootcamp coaching team" (owner, 2026-10-12), in HTML and plain text alike. Emails speak as "we", never "I". Subjects state the fact ("You're booked: {date} at {time}"), not the pitch. HTML and plain-text bodies must say the same thing.
- **Metadata / OG descriptions**: one or two sentences, geography + mechanic, no scarcity theatrics. These are the coach's handshake in a search result.
