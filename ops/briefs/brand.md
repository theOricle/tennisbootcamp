# Brand Brief — TennisBootcamp.ca

## Tone
Welcoming, serious, athletic, premium. The voice should feel like a world-class coach speaking directly to a motivated player — not a salesperson and not a tech company.

## What to Avoid
- SaaS or tech-network visual language (gradients, floating UI cards, glassmorphism for its own sake)
- Generic startup design patterns
- Visual clutter or competing calls-to-action
- Extra CTAs that dilute the primary conversion goal
- Anything that looks like it belongs on a productivity app

## Hero Section
The hero should feel dynamic and high-performance. If animation is used, it must feel tennis-related — ball physics, racket motion, court geometry — never abstract tech motion. The hero's job is to communicate elite training at first glance and drive the primary CTA.

## CTA Hierarchy
Updated 2026-11-17 (backlog #69) to the owner's 2026-11-16 decision: groups are open to join, so the main button takes a player to the groups, and the quiz is the side path. The button's label is "Join a bootcamp" (owner, 2026-11-17, backlog #70; it read "Join a group" until then).

1. **Join a bootcamp** (primary — the lime button on every public page, links to `/programs`)
2. **Take the 2-minute quiz** (the side line beside the main button: "Not sure which group? Take the 2-minute quiz" — a text link to `/intake`, never a lime button)
3. **Newsletter signup** (tertiary — one line at the top of the footer, low-friction, no competition with primary)

"Book Your Assessment" is the exact label of the optional $20 assessment button (program pages, the quiz's last screen, the dashboard). It is never the primary CTA. "Browse Programs" stays as an outline link to the catalog where a page keeps one. "Get Priority Placement" and "Find My Program" are retired labels.

## Visual Direction
- Athletic and clean: generous whitespace, strong typography, high-contrast accents
- Court colors and textures are appropriate when used with restraint
- Photography or motion should feature real tennis action, not staged lifestyle shots
- No stock-photo feel
